The garden plan we can build

Ruth Sykes' proposal · shared Oct 17, 2026

Ruth Sykes — coordinator of the Mary Esther Community Garden — sent the City and the team a full garden plan on Oct 17: what the garden is for, how it's laid out, how it gets watered, and what grows in it. It's the working blueprint for this site, and it's what the Phase 2 layout drawing gets drawn from.

Grow food for members & food banks Teach gardening & nutrition Bring neighbors together A peaceful place to recharge Pollinators, compost & careful water use

Garden map

Planned · Not yet built

Planned layout from the garden plan, not yet built. A landscape plan drawing made from Ruth Sykes' written plan, showing where each part of the garden is intended to go. The final layout still gets adapted to the tennis center site and approved by the City in Phase 2.

Entrance and gathering area: by the gate at the bottom of the map, with the archway sign, notice board, benches, shaded picnic tables under the pergola, and room to meet.

  • Raised beds, 4 × 10 ft: wood framed beds with soil and crop rows, for members to rent or adopt.
  • Shared plots: group projects and food pantry donations.
  • Accessible beds: raised to wheelchair height, at the path edge near a main path.
  • Children's garden: easy crops and sensory plants, in brighter rounded beds.
  • Fruit and berries: canopy fruit trees with blueberries, strawberries, and blackberries.
  • Pollinator strip: marigolds, sunflowers, lavender, zinnias, milkweed, and wildflowers along the fence.
  • Shed, greenhouse, cold frames: tools, seed starting, and a longer season.
  • Compost and rainwater: three bin compost, with barrels and a tank fed by roof runoff.
  • Pergola and gathering: slatted shade, picnic tables with umbrellas, benches, and notice board.
  • Hose stations: blue H markers are spigots spaced through the garden.
  • Drip irrigation: light blue dashed lines run along the raised bed rows.
  • Paths and fence: warm gravel paths inside a posts and rails fence, with one entrance gate.

Sizes and positions are a planning guide, not a survey. The drawing follows Ruth's written plan only, and the layout still needs fitting to the site and City approval.

ArrivalEntrance & gathering area

  • Archway sign with the garden's name at the gate
  • Notice board for announcements, volunteer schedules, and events
  • Benches and shaded picnic tables for meetings and relaxing

Growing spaceThe beds

  • Individual raised beds, 4 × 10 ft, for members to rent or adopt
  • Shared community plots for group projects and food pantry donations
  • Accessible beds at wheelchair height for seniors and limited mobility
  • A children's garden with easy-to-grow crops and sensory plants

BackboneStructures

  • Tool shed stocked with shovels, rakes, gloves, watering cans, and carts
  • Greenhouse or cold frames for seed starting and a longer season
  • Composting station for garden waste and kitchen scraps
  • Rainwater collection — barrels or cisterns fed by roof runoff
  • Pergola / shade pavilion for rest and workshops
  • Fence and gate for security and to deter wildlife

WaterWatering the garden

  • Rainwater harvested from the shed and pavilion roofs
  • Drip irrigation in the raised beds for efficient watering
  • Mulch around plants to hold moisture in the soil
  • Hose stations and spigots spaced throughout the garden

PlantingsWhat we'll grow

  • Vegetables: tomatoes, peppers, cucumbers, squash, lettuce, beans, carrots — rotated by season for soil health
  • Herbs: basil, parsley, mint, oregano, thyme, rosemary
  • Fruit: blueberries, strawberries, blackberries, and other fruit suited to this area
  • For pollinators: marigolds, sunflowers, lavender, zinnias, milkweed, and native wildflowers
  • Perennials & natives: low-maintenance planting for beauty and biodiversity

Built to lastSustainability touches

  • Solar lighting for safety
  • Recycled materials for pathways and raised beds
  • Educational signs explaining composting, irrigation, and plant life cycles
  • A habitat corner — birdhouses, bee hotels, and a butterfly garden

Once it's growingPrograms in the plan

  • Workshops on gardening, composting, and nutrition
  • Volunteer days to care for the shared areas
  • Harvest festivals and farmers' markets
  • School partnerships for youth education and service projects
  • Donation program supplying fresh produce to local food banks and shelters

How the plan moves the checklist

  • →The plan itself gives Phase 2 its starting point — "Draw garden layout" is no longer a blank page, and the City approves a real layout.
  • →Hose stations + drip irrigation are what the City's water lines and spigot tasks in Phase 3 get built to serve.
  • →4 × 10 ft beds, fence & gate, entrance sign match the sponsorable items already on the wish list — a sponsor can fund a piece of the plan by name.
  • →Workshops & school partnerships become the garden's first education program in Phase 5.
